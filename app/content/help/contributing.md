Bardic Torch gathers the bardic community's scattered songbooks, playlists and album pages into one findable index. You don't have to be a bard to help, and every contribution counts.

:::infobox{title="At a glance"}

- **Who can contribute:** anyone with an account (log in with Google or Facebook).
- **What we collect:** links, credits, categories and sourced notes. Never lyrics, files, audio or video.
- **Review:** a moderator checks every contributor's edit before it goes live.
- **Licence:** your contributions are released under CC BY-SA 4.0.

:::

:::note

The catalogue is on its way. Until it opens here, you can browse every piece on the [original Bardic Torch wiki](https://bardictorch.miraheze.org/wiki/Category:Pieces). Editing on this site arrives in a later release; this guide explains the rules it will follow.

:::

## Our principles

1. **Any contribution, however small, is welcome.** A bard page with one song, or a piece with a title and a single link, is a good start. Someone else can add more later.
2. **As many links as possible.** Websites go down and paper gets lost in attics. A fifth lyrics link, or a Bandcamp link alongside YouTube and Spotify, is still useful.
3. **Links only.** Bardic Torch is an index, not an archive. We never host lyrics or the text of a work, not even public-domain or Creative Commons text. We don't accept uploads of files, audio or video. Every piece points to where it already lives.
4. **No private information.** Stick to what bards publish on their own websites, blogs and album pages. A public social media page under a public name is fine. Posts from private groups are not.
5. **Sources for licensing and etiquette.** Any statement about permissions, licences or a bard's wishes needs a link to a public page where the creator said it. This applies even if you are the bard (see [why your word isn't enough](/help/faq-for-bards)).

## Before you add something

Check that the bard or piece isn't already listed. Search for every name variant you can think of:

- for bards: their SCA persona name, stage name, nickname or band name
- for pieces: the shortest distinctive part of the title. Search for "Knight McKnight" rather than "The Daring Exploits of Knight McKnight", so you catch every version of the title
- the author's list of works

If you know a piece by another title, don't create a second entry. Add your title as an **alias** of the existing one, so the next person can find it by either name.

## What a good piece entry has

You only need to add what you know. The fullest entries have:

- **Key links**: the official or canonical home of the piece, such as the author's own blog post, album page or sheet music. For historical works, the original source.
- **Lyrics and text links**: songbooks, archives and websites that host the words. Links only.
- **Performances and recordings**: links to videos, streams and albums, each with a rich description. Say who performs it, at which event, in what year, who uploaded it and on which site. For example: "Performance on YouTube, uploaded by So-and-so, of the song performed by Such-and-such at Event Name in 2026". If a link dies, that description is how someone finds the recording again.
- **Licensing and etiquette**: what the creator allows, such as free performance, permission on request, or a wish that the piece is never written down. Every statement needs a link to the public page where the creator said it.
- **Categories**: as many as apply (see below).

## Credits

- Credit people by **the name they publish under**, whether that's an SCA name, a stage name or a legal name. If they already have a page, use the same name it uses.
- You can credit a bard who doesn't have a page yet. Their name becomes a **stub** page that anyone can help complete.
- Only add a legal or mundane name if the bard publishes it themselves. Even then, it stays hidden until a moderator has checked it.

## Categories and naming

Categories (here called **terms**) let people find pieces by what they need. Each term belongs to a facet:

- **Genre**: the kind of piece. Songs (sung), Instrumental (playable from sheet music), Stories (narrative spoken pieces), Poems (not set to music; don't tag songs as poems just because they rhyme), Dances and Comedy. A piece can be both a song and an instrumental, or both a story and a poem.
- **Mood**: the performance context it suits, not every shade of feeling. Tragic for a funeral, Comedic or Joyful for a party, Warlike for the battlefield, Relaxed for a late-night campfire, and so on. Narrower moods are for contexts, such as marching songs under Warlike.
- **Topic**: the main subjects only, not everything mentioned. Keep them broad and put specific ones underneath, so Swords goes under War.
- **Culture**: the persona or setting a piece suits, such as Norse pieces for a Viking persona. It doesn't describe the bard's background or the language of the piece. List from broad to specific: European, British, English.
- **Language**: languages other than English.
- **Legendaria**: cycles of legend, such as Arthuriana or the Fenian Cycle.
- **Origin** (the wiki called it "Source Category"): where a piece comes from. The terms are Pre-1600 Sources (documentably), Traditional & Folk, Borrowings (modern songs from outside the community; take extra care with copyright), SCA Pieces, SF&F Pieces, Contrafacta (new words to an old tune), Contrafacta (Modern) (new words to a tune still in copyright) and Setting Pieces (new music for old words). Century terms such as 16th Century sit under Origin and mean "documentable to". A piece can have more than one origin, with one marked as the main one. Please don't use "filk": it means too many different things.
- **Conservation**: how safe a piece is from being lost (see [Conservation status](/help/conservation-status)).

### Naming a new term

- Use natural English and capitalise the content words: **Green Eggs & Ham**, not GreenEggsAndHam, "green eggs n ham" or Green Eggs/Ham.
- The only punctuation allowed is `( )`, `'` and `&`.
- If the same word means different things in different facets, add a suffix in brackets: **Welsh (Language)** beside the Welsh culture, **SCA (Topic)** beside SCA Pieces.
- Every new term needs a **parent**, such as Topics or War. Suggested terms are reviewed along with the edit that uses them.

## How review works

Every edit from a contributor waits for a moderator before it goes live. The moderators are currently John and Fabian. This is a normal step: it keeps copyright problems and unsourced claims off the site.

- While your edit waits, you see your own version, with a banner saying it's pending. Everyone else sees the last approved version.
- Moderators' own edits go live straight away, but they are recorded in the page history like everyone else's.
- Every page keeps a public history.
