Bardic communities have customs that standard licences don’t cover. You might be happy for anyone to sing your song from memory, but not want it written down. You might love sing-alongs on the chorus but not on the verses. The Bard Preference Checklist helps you say all of that in one place.

:::infobox{title="At a glance"}

- **What it is:** a long list of preferences a bard might have, grouped into 8 sections
- **Who it’s for:** anyone who writes or performs bardic work
- **How to use it:** copy the list, delete what doesn’t apply, and publish the rest on your own site
- **Time needed:** as much as you like; start with the sections that matter most to you
- **Full list:** [on the original wiki](https://bardictorch.miraheze.org/wiki/Bard_Preference_Checklist)

:::

## What it is

The checklist is a list of everything you might want to put on a preferences page. That’s a page you might call your licensing FAQ, etiquette page or permissions page.

Copy the [full checklist from the original wiki](https://bardictorch.miraheze.org/wiki/Bard_Preference_Checklist) into your own notes and delete everything that doesn’t apply to you. Or write your page from scratch and use the list to check you haven’t missed anything. If you have a preference that isn’t on the list, include it anyway.

Then **publish it on your own website, blog or album page** and send us the link. We’ll link to it from your bard page and summarise it for readers.

:::note

We can’t host your preferences as original text, because we can’t check who wrote them. A page you publish yourself is the source that every summary on Bardic Torch points back to.

:::

## Why it matters

People want to respect your wishes, but they can only do that if they know what those wishes are. Right now they’re often scattered across comment threads, liner notes and video descriptions.

One clear page means:

- performers know whether to ask, credit you or tell you afterwards
- songbook compilers know whether they may print your words
- everyone can check your wishes before recording, adapting or selling your work

If all this is more detail than you need, you can simply name a [Creative Commons licence](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/), such as CC BY-NC-SA. If you say nothing at all, the default copyright law in your country applies.

## What the checklist covers

The checklist has 8 sections. Here are some of the options in each.

### 1. Licensing and copyright

How your work is legally released:

- all in the public domain
- under a Creative Commons licence (BY, BY-SA, BY-NC, BY-NC-SA, BY-ND or BY-NC-ND)
- all rights reserved
- under another licence you link to
- it varies, so check each work

If you use a Creative Commons licence, don’t add rules that contradict it.

### 2. Performance permission

Whether others may perform your work:

- no, and please don’t ask
- yes if they ask you through a contact method you name, perhaps with conditions, or after performing it to you first
- through your label or group
- yes for a particular group, such as SCA bards at SCA events
- yes, freely given

You can also say whether you want to **hear about performances**. That could mean you must be told, you’d love to know, it doesn’t matter, or you’d rather not know.

### 3. Adaptations

How you feel about people remixing or changing your work:

- encouraged, or simply allowed
- allowed with conditions, such as only minor changes, no parodies of serious pieces, or translating and localising but not ‘improving’
- ask first, and show me before you perform it
- live performance only, no uploads
- please don’t

You can say whether you want to **be told about adaptations**, and which **kinds** you’re happy with. Examples include new words to your tune, fan videos, changing a story’s plot, shortening or extending a piece, setting a poem to music, and using it in a play.

### 4. Attribution

How you’d like to be credited, which can differ by context:

- live performance: casual (a campfire or bardic circle), formal (a competition, court or free stage), semi-commercial (busking) or commercial (a paid or ticketed show)
- print: a free songbook or handout, or something sold, such as a book, poster or T-shirt
- online: someone uploading a recording of you, or of their own performance of your work, with or without making money
- advertising for a show or album that includes your work

For each context you can say **what** to credit and **how**. What could be your SCA name, stage name or legal name, an affiliation, or where to find you online. How could be verbal credit before or after, once per set, a sign or QR code, or best judgement.

There’s also space for sensitive notes. You might want an important preference repeated with every credit, or to keep a former name or your location private. You can also separate credit you legally require from credit you’d simply like.

### 5. Commercial use

A list of money-related activities, each of which you can allow or not:

- busking, paid gigs and ticketed shows
- uploads with optional tips, streaming royalties, download and CD sales
- selling printed copies or merchandise
- using your work in advertising

Each one can carry conditions, such as asking first, telling you, offering you first refusal, sharing profits, or amateurs only.

### 6. Recording and writing down

Whether your work may be captured:

- not at all
- performed but never written down, kept in the oral folk tradition
- written in a personal bard book but not shared
- shared in person but not online
- lyrics online are fine, but not recordings
- uploads are fine

You can also say whether people may **record you while you perform**. Options include no, ask first, tell me afterwards, or formal venues only and never at bardic circles.

### 7. Performance etiquette

The customs of the circle:

- **sing-along**: join in, join in on the chorus only, sing the melody but no harmonies, no clapping or drumming, or please don’t
- **competitions**: don’t compete with my work, only if I’m not entering, check with me first, or not on the opposing side
- **bardic circles where I’m present**: fine, check with me beforehand, a quick word is enough, ask only if you know me, or don’t
- **learning my work**: any way you like, off-book only, or practise it thoroughly first

### 8. Objectionable use

Purposes, contexts and content you don’t want your work linked to. Examples include political or religious campaigning, hateful groups, advertising certain products, events that serve alcohol, or content that isn’t suitable for children.

You can also say what should happen if someone is unsure. They might ask you, tell you so you can act, or leave it to you rather than speaking on your behalf.

## How Bardic Torch will show your preferences

:::note

Coming later. For now, each piece’s licensing card has a short note about its creator’s wishes.

:::

Bard pages will show an **etiquette card** that summarises your preferences in plain language. The same card will appear on the page for each of your pieces, so people see your wishes where they need them.

- **Every point links to its source**, your own published page, so readers can check your exact words.
- **Piece-level rules win.** If one song has different rules from the rest of your work, the song’s own rules take precedence on its page.
- **It changes when you do.** Update your page, send us the new link, and the card will be updated once a moderator has checked it.

To get started, publish your page and [send us the link](https://forms.gle/wUwWnMKtWZSUisWCA). For more about sources and opting out, see the [FAQ for bards](/help/faq-for-bards).
