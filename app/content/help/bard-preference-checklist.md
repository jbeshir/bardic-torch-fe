## What is this page?

This checklist aims to be a comprehensive list of everything you might want to include on a bardic preferences page (aka licensing FAQ, etiquette page, permissions page, etc).

### Why Create a Bard Preference Page?

One of the ways that you, as a bard, can really help Bardic Torch is by creating a page that summarises information about your legal rights, licensing, etiquette preferences, performance preferences, and other how-to-interact-with-this-bard’s-work instructions.

We want to make sure that people respect your wishes to the greatest extent possible – and that’s easiest to do when people know what they are. That’s hard to do when information is scattered across Facebook comments, album liner notes and YouTube descriptions.

We cannot host unsourced, original information on Bardic Torch. If you create a page that states your wishes, we can link to that page from your profile here so that the information is always available and easy to find.

Creators in the bardic community sometimes have requests that are, outside of the bardic community, relatively unusual; they might, for example, be okay with others performing their work so long as they do it from memory and don’t write it down. This means that just using standard licences like Creative Commons often doesn’t capture everything we might want to know about a bard’s instructions.

### How can I use this page?

This page is trying to be an exhaustive list of every preference a bard _might_ have. If you have licensing decisions, preferences, or beliefs around etiquette which you don’t see on this page – add them!

The idea is that you can copy-paste this entire list to your own notes, then delete everything that does not apply to you. Or you can totally rewrite your page from scratch, but use this page as a reference to make sure that you’ve covered _everything_ you might want to specify.

If this is way more detailed than you care for, and you want an easy version, check out the list of [Creative Commons licences](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/). If you state that your work is released under (for example) CC-BY-NC-SA, you’ll be using an easy-to-follow guide that many other creators use in the same way. You can also say nothing at all – in which case your work will be protected by the default copyright law in your country.

## The Checklist

### Licensing & Copyright

My works are legally... (delete as appropriate)

- all released into the public domain
- released under a Creative Commons licence
  - List of Creative Commons licences: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/list.en>
  - List of Creative Commons licence codes: <https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/>
    - CC BY: it’s okay to do anything with the work so long as you credit the creator (give attribution)
    - CC BY-SA: it’s okay to do anything with the work so long as you credit the creator (give attribution) and share any adaptations under the same CC BY-SA licence
    - CC BY-NC: it’s okay to use the work for noncommercial purposes only, so long as you credit the creator (give attribution)
    - CC BY-NC-SA: it’s okay to use the work for noncommercial purposes only, so long as you credit the creator (give attribution) and share your adaptations under the same CC BY-NC-SA licence
    - CC BY-ND: it’s okay to copy and distribute the work so long as you don’t adapt or change it and you give credit (attribution) to the creator
    - CC BY-NC-ND: it’s okay to copy and distribute the work for noncommercial purposes only, so long as you don’t adapt or change it, and you give credit (attribution) to the creator
- all copyrighted with all rights reserved
- released under a specific legal licence which I will link to
- released under a variety of different licences; some might be public domain, while others might be released under Creative Commons, and others might be copyrighted with all rights reserved. Please check the individual work/album page for details.

If you use a Creative Commons licence, you should stick to the terms of the licence – don’t specify contradictory rules to what the licence says.

### Adaptations
